The LPs get their capital back today, and pay for it with upside

A new investor, NLP, puts $35M into the fund. That money returns the capital the LPs are still owed — completing 1x now rather than whenever the portfolio sells. In exchange NLP takes about a seventh of all future profit, bought at a discount, and is repaid before anyone else.

Where the fund stands

$11M has been distributed and $35M of LP capital is still outstanding, against a portfolio carried at $360M. So the value is there on paper; what the LPs do not have is cash, and nobody controls when the companies actually sell.

The two phases meet exactly: NLP is whole at the moment the fund has distributed $35M, and takes nothing extra on the way. That is the whole economic design — first money back, then pari passu.

Why the paperwork looks complicated

Only a tenth of NLP's cheque can move offshore-to-offshore into the fund. The other 90% has to come onshore in India and buy shares in the companies directly. So NLP ends up holding two positions — a small interest in the fund, and a direct stake in each portfolio company — and where it cannot hold shares directly, a put/call written into the company's shareholder agreement gives it the same economics.

Everything else in the term sheet is bookkeeping to make those two positions behave as one investor: NLP gets its $35M, then its 14%, no matter which route the money travels.

What still needs deciding

1

The put/call at each company. It is how NLP's India vehicle participates where it cannot hold shares — so its enforceability matters more than it looks, and each company's other shareholders have to consent.

2

Getting money from the offshore vehicle to the onshore one. Both are NLP, so it is an internal transfer — but most of the repayment lands offshore, and the route and its tax treatment have to be papered rather than assumed.

3

Who bears the tax on the onshore purchase. Currently assumed to be grossed up. That assumption moves the size of NLP's stake, and therefore every split.

4

The GP's own unreturned capital. A small amount, and the $35M as structured does not fund it. Either the cheque is grossed up or the GP is paid ahead of Phase 2.
